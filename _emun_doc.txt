1. Project Overview
Emun is a marketplace platform designed to make it easy for people to list items for sale and for buyers to 
discover, compare, and contact sellers in one place. The platform can support major categories such as 
homes, phones, computers, vehicles, appliances, furniture, and general goods.
The main value proposition is convenience: users should be able to create listings quickly, search with 
confidence, and communicate without friction. For a side project, the product should remain focused on the  
core transaction journey instead of trying to solve every marketplace problem on day one.
1.1 Concept and Objectives
Create a trusted space where people can post items for sale and browse offers by category, location, 
and price.
Reduce the effort required to find relevant listings through good search, filters, and recommendation 
logic.
Provide a lightweight broker experience that helps buyers and sellers connect without unnecessary 
steps.
Build a product that can start small as an MVP and grow into a multi-category marketplace over time.
1.2 Target Users and Market Needs
Individual sellers who want a simple way to advertise personal items.
Small businesses and dealers who need regular listings and lead generation.
Buyers who want faster discovery, better filtering, and direct contact with sellers.
Administrators or moderators who need to manage listings, reported content, and trust issues.
Typical market needs include better discovery, better trust, and faster interaction. Most marketplace users 
do not want a complicated checkout flow for every item; they want a practical way to find an item, assess it,  
and contact the seller quickly.
2. Core Features
The initial feature set should be shaped around the listing lifecycle: create, discover, contact, and manage.
2.1 User Registration and Profiles
Sign up and login using email, phone number, or social authentication.
Basic profile fields such as name, avatar, location, bio, and contact preferences.
Seller profile page showing active listings, past listings, and rating or trust indicators.
Optional verification badges for phone number, email, and identity later in the roadmap.
2.2 Item Listing and Categorization
Create listing form with title, description, price, category, subcategory, condition, location, and photos.
Support for draft saving so sellers can finish later.
Category hierarchy and tags to improve search and browsing.
Promoted attributes for specific item types, for example bedrooms for homes or RAM/storage for 
phones and PCs.
2.3 Search and Filtering System
Keyword search across title, description, category, and tags.
Filters for price range, location, condition, category, date posted, and seller type.
Sorting by newest, price low to high, price high to low, and relevance.
Saved searches and favorites for returning users.

2.4 Messaging or Contact System
In-app chat for buyer-seller communication.
Alternative contact options such as tap-to-call or WhatsApp link where appropriate.
Read receipts, message timestamps, and spam controls.
Message moderation and reporting tools for abusive or fraudulent behavior.
2.5 Payment or Transaction Facilitation
For a marketplace like Emun, full in-app payment can be introduced later. The MVP can start with lead 
generation and direct contact. If transactions are later handled in-app, the platform can support escrow, 
service fees, featured payment requests, and transaction status tracking.
Phase 1: contact-only marketplace with no embedded payment.
Phase 2: optional deposit or reservation payment for selected categories.
Phase 3: full marketplace payments, commissions, and transaction history.

3.3 Data Model Overview
Users: account details, profile data, verification flags, roles, and settings.
Listings: title, description, category, location, price, condition, visibility, and timestamps.
Listing images: upload metadata, ordering, and storage references.
Categories: hierarchical structure for goods, subcategories, and attribute templates.
Messages and conversations: participant IDs, message content, timestamps, and read state.
Favorites and saved searches: improve retention and repeat visits.
Reports and moderation events: support trust and safety operations.
4. User Experience and UI
The UI should feel fast, low friction, and trustworthy. Marketplace products succeed when the path from 
search to contact is obvious and the list of actions on every screen is small.
4.1 Design Principles
Mobile-first layouts with responsive expansion for web.
Clean card-based listing presentation with strong photo hierarchy.
Minimal form steps when creating a listing.
High contrast for price, location, and action buttons.
Trust signals such as verification, seller identity, and report controls.
4.2 Key Screens
Onboarding and authentication screens.
Home feed with featured listings and category shortcuts.
Search results with filters and sorting.
Listing details with gallery, seller info, and contact actions.
Create / edit listing flow.
Chat or contact inbox.
Profile and settings.
Admin/moderation dashboard.
4.3 Core User Flow
Buyer journey: open app -> search or browse -> filter listings -> view item details -> contact seller -> 
save or share listing.
Seller journey: sign in -> create listing -> upload photos -> publish -> answer inquiries -> mark item as 
sold.
Admin journey: review reports -> remove spam or suspicious content -> manage categories and 
featured content.

6. Development Plan
The safest way to build Emun is to define a narrow MVP, ship it, validate demand, and expand only after 
users prove which flows matter.
6.1 MVP Definition
User registration and login.
Profile creation for buyers and sellers.
Create and manage listings with photos.
Browse listings by category.
Search and basic filters.
Listing detail page with contact actions.
Favorites or saved listings.
Admin tools for moderation and category management.
6.2 Step-by-Step Roadmap
1.Step 1: define the exact target market, category focus, and launch region.
2.Step 2: create wireframes and a minimal design system.
3.Step 3: implement auth, profiles, listing creation, and media upload.
4.Step 4: build search, categories, and listing detail pages.
5.Step 5: add messaging or contact actions and basic moderation.
6.Step 6: test on real devices, fix usability issues, and improve performance.
7.Step 7: deploy beta, collect user feedback, and monitor analytics.
8.Step 8: expand features such as saved searches, notifications, and monetization.

7. Deployment and Scaling
For a side project, keep infrastructure simple and inexpensive at the start. Optimize only when the product 
has real usage.
7.1 Hosting Options
Frontend hosting: Vercel, Netlify, Firebase Hosting, or a static hosting bucket.
Backend hosting: Render, Railway, Fly.io, DigitalOcean App Platform, or a small VPS.
Database hosting: managed PostgreSQL on Supabase, Neon, RDS, or cloud provider equivalents.
Media storage: S3-compatible storage such as AWS S3, Cloudflare R2, or Supabase Storage.
7.2 Scaling Considerations
Move from database search to a dedicated search service when listings grow.
Introduce caching for home feed, categories, and hot listings.
Use background workers for image processing, notifications, and cleanup jobs.
Separate read-heavy endpoints from write-heavy ones if traffic grows.
Add monitoring, logs, and alerting early so production issues are visible.
8. Security and Legal Considerations
Even a small marketplace needs clear safety rules. Trust and legal clarity are part of the product, not an 
afterthought.
Protect accounts with secure password handling, token expiration, rate limiting, and optional two-factor  
authentication.
Sanitize inputs and validate file uploads to reduce injection and malicious content risks.
Provide reporting, blocking, and moderation tools for spam, scams, and abusive users.
Log sensitive events such as login attempts, listing changes, and moderation actions.
Create Terms of Service, Privacy Policy, and acceptable use rules before public launch.
Be careful with local regulations for real estate, financial transactions, and identity verification.
If payments are introduced, review KYC/AML, chargeback handling, and dispute procedures.
9. Practical Recommendation for Emun
For a side project, the strongest first version of Emun is a mobile-first marketplace with a web companion, 
not a full financial marketplace on day one. Start with listings, search, filters, seller profiles, and direct 
contact. That gives you something valuable, testable, and realistic to ship.
A strong implementation path would be Flutter for the app, PostgreSQL for data, a modular backend API, 
and S3-compatible storage for images. Once the core usage is proven, add monetization features such as 
featured listings and seller subscriptions.
